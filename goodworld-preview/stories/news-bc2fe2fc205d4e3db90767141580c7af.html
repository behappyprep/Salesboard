Even modest daily walks are linked to slower memory decline.

In a long-term observational study, walking at least 3,000 steps a day was associated with more favorable Alzheimer's-related brain measures.

Researchers followed nearly 300 older adults for roughly a decade and tracked their activity and brain health. Among people who already showed early Alzheimer's-related changes, greater daily movement was associated with slower tau buildup and cognitive decline. Benefits appeared at around 3,000 steps a day and were stronger at moderate amounts of walking. Because the study was observational, it cannot prove that walking alone caused the difference.
