OUR EDITORIAL STANDARDS

Trust is more important than a good headline.

A positive-news publication should be encouraging without exaggerating what has actually happened.

Primary-source reporting

We prioritise sources that can be identified and checked, such as official agencies, national parks, research institutions and established animal-welfare organisations. We link the announcement or report behind each story. We do not invent quotes, scientific results or outcomes.

Evidence and health reporting

We distinguish an approved treatment from an experimental study, an early clinical trial from established care, and a measured conservation outcome from a future plan. Articles about medical care are for news and general information, not personal medical advice. Readers should confirm clinical details through the original regulator or research institution.

Automation and editorial responsibility

Software helps discover, check, queue, summarize and publish news. Automated checks reduce errors but cannot guarantee that every claim is correct. Longer editorial articles are treated separately from brief, automatically prepared updates. We do not describe a short source-based summary as original on-the-ground reporting.

Photography and licensing

We use source photographs only where reuse rights are confirmed. Otherwise, we may use a clearly marked, freely licensed representative photograph. Each photo has a visible attribution and license link. A representative photo is not evidence of the specific event in the story.

Translations

Most translated articles are generated by open-source machine translation software. They can contain errors, particularly with drug names and scientific terminology. The primary-source link remains available in every language.

Corrections

Readers can report a factual error, image-rights issue or broken source. A correction is reviewed against the original source. When a claim cannot be substantiated, the story may be corrected, relabelled or withdrawn. We do not silently treat an outdated source as a new medical approval.
